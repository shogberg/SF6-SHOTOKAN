# Coach Coosco: replay breakdowns

Source: Coosco (twitch.tv/coosco, metafy.gg/@coosco), who reviews viewer-submitted SF6 replays live every Monday and posts them to YouTube. Paraphrased in my own words with timestamps so the video can be checked.

Videos covered:
- **V1** "Your Opponent Is Telling You What They'll Do | SF6 Replay Review" (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KvdwO6qz5V0, uploaded 2026-11-08, 49 min). Three replays: Yasmin vs Luke (2:02), Cammy vs Crimson Viper (18:40), Cammy vs Ken (38:13). Two of the three are Cammy, so they apply directly to {{PLAYER_NAME}}.

Coosco's core theme: the opponent leaves a paper trail of clues about what they'll do next. Stay consistent (keep things airtight, take fewer hard reads) and walk forward instead of turtling or jumping.

When adding a new Coosco video, add it to the list above as V2, V3... and tag new points with its id.

## 1. Walk forward before you press ("knocking on the door")
- **Flirting / messaging (V1 4:38).** Pressing a button at the opponent (knocking on the door) tempts a knee-jerk button back. When the opponent knocks on *your* door, don't answer from where you stand: once their button is out it isn't your turn. Walk forward a little, then place your button, so it actually reaches and you aren't whiff-punished (5:24, 18:15).
- **Walk forward is the default approach (V1 8:30, 17:43).** Jumping as the main approach opens you to anti-airs and to buttons like Luke's Sand Blast. Walking forward lets you block the fireball, parry it on reaction to the hand coming up, and keep closing distance. A jump that gets stuffed resets you to the start.
- **Jumping is good but must be responsible (V1 10:16).** If you do jump in, pick the button that gives the most (e.g. a heavy for plus frames and a combo), especially on low-damage characters.
- **Don't turtle when you feel pressure (V1 7:07).** After a scary exchange, players start down-backing and let the opponent get comfortable. When you notice you haven't acted for a while, reset yourself: walk forward and initiate your gameplan. Turtling only works if it's *strategic* patience: block a string out and take your turn when they go minus. Patience that means pressing nothing for 15 seconds backfires (V1 45:43, 47:45).
- **Walk forward to fight out of the corner (V1 24:44).** Players get hesitant with their back to the wall, but the opponent can't have the turn forever. Step forward and place a button (e.g. a long-range normal) to push them back. That makes room to walk back yourself, so their knee-jerk button whiffs and you can whiff punish, jump over or swap positions. Coosco points to Dual Kevin at Evo France as an example of wiggling out without overextending.
- **Walk back after pokes (V1 29:46).** After landing a poke in neutral, walk back a bit or down-back. If they answer with 2MK~Drive Rush it often won't reach. If they do it anyway, that's data: next time down-back, make them spend 3 Drive bars, then block their mixup or reversal it.

## 2. Read the clues the opponent leaves
- **Dropped or weak combos (V1 6:19, 19:59).** When the opponent drops a combo or gets a hit but little reward, they often feel cheated and overextend right after: jump, press an extra button, whiff. Count your blessings, then expect that overextension and be ready (anti-air, whiff punish, block).
- **Missed whiff punishes break minds (V1 41:11).** A player whose whiff punish was too slow often goes into "red alert": point-blank heavies, repeated whiffs, neutral skips (e.g. Ken's Dragonlash). These are clues they're about to leave themselves open.
- **First-time events trigger responses (V1 32:29).** The first time you land something in a set (e.g. the first whiff punish), watch how they react next, such as an immediate wake-up tech or reversal. They're human and things on screen trigger them.
- **Your own frustrating moments are readable too (V1 46:14).** After something annoying happens to you (e.g. you parried the first hit but ate the second), a wake-up OD DP is the obvious response and good opponents call it out. Down-back there instead.
- **Repeated whiffs mean more whiffs (V1 44:30).** If they've shown they whiff at a range once, they'll do it again. Don't over-focus on the exact button; just expect a whiff and create space (walk back) to punish it instead of walking into it.

## 3. Don't give up your turn
- **Throw after a minus button (V1 11:13).** Drive Rush plus frames → light → light → walk up throw gives up your turn, because the second light (e.g. 2LK) is minus and walking forward lets them wiggle out. Only do this against a cornered opponent who can't move.
- **Point-blank unsafe normals (V1 3:05).** Using punishable mediums and heavies point blank (Yasmin 5MK, 5HK in the example) hands over the turn. Use them further away; up close, throw, shimmy or jab.
- **Forcing it when not plus enough (V1 14:26).** If your move isn't plus enough (e.g. a blocked boomerang), walk forward and see how they react instead of forcing another committal special.
- **Mid-range throws (V1 42:44).** If you aren't shimmying, don't down-back before throwing midscreen; just walk forward and throw. Defensive throw timing is for when they're cornered and can't move.
- **Throw then Drive Rush is fake for Cammy (V1 30:56).** Unlike Ken or Terry, Cammy's midscreen throw into Drive Rush gives no real mixup, so people OD DP it. To keep pressure after a midscreen throw, Coosco uses forward dash into back+MP (he says about +5 in his demo; check current frame data), or other buttons that put her right on top of them, then a drill, jabs, or 5HK if further away.
- **Drive Rush 2LK belongs in neutral (V1 28:39).** Use Drive Rush 2LK in neutral to clip people walking or pressing. After a throw they're already down-backing, so it isn't a mixup; use a more plus button there instead.

## 4. Cammy specifics (V1 replays #2 and #3)
- **After a drill (Spiral Arrow) that leaves you plus but far (V1 19:05, 39:35).** Walk forward a little after the dash before pressing, or use Drive Rush 5HP / back+MP, so your follow-up (jab, shimmy, throw, 2HP) is actually in range. Coosco sees this a lot: drill, dash up, no walk forward, so the 2HP isn't threatening and the jab whiffs.
- **Don't Drive Rush at round start (V1 38:28).** He says he's essentially never done it as Cammy. Her Drive Rush isn't strong compared with Ken, Terry or Luke, and 5HP is 8 frames and gets beaten. If you must, Drive Rush jab. Being random is fine elsewhere, but round start is too volatile.
- **Anti-air with DP against a burn-kicker (V1 22:01).** Against a Viper who likes Burn Kick, anti-air with Cannon Spike.
- **Spend 2 Drive for corner carry (V1 43:37).** Even after a dropped confirm, spending 2 bars (OD drill) to get pressure and put them at the wall is worth it.

## 5. Corner: suffocate them (V1 35:24)
- A back throw that puts them in the corner is huge ("count your blessings"); make the most of it. Walk forward and put normals on them to push them even closer, then run strike/throw/shimmy.
- Don't walk back waiting for them to do something; that lets them out for free. In a final round with good position, a jump-in isn't needed and gives them an out. Keep it airtight.
- Act fast: waiting too long to press (e.g. a late 5HK) lets them jump out and swap the corner (36:36).
- **Both players fishing (V1 37:08).** At low life both players fish for the one clean hit (a buffered Level 3, a jab counter-hit into a kill). Prioritising the corner pressure earlier avoids getting there.

## 6. Committal tools: use them responsibly
- **Hard-read round starts (V1 2:19).** A slow special at round start (Yasmin's boomerang) locks you in an animation while you don't know what they'll do, e.g. a Drive Rush. For round starts, walk forward and see how they react: 5LK their Drive Rush, jab for a counter hit as they get close, or a delayed forward jump.
- **Drive Impact (V1 12:28).** It won't pay off consistently, and looking for DI chances pulls attention away from the rest of your game. His own example: fishing for DI against Manon's overhead led to DIs on jumps and getting counter-DI'd. Use DI even more sparingly than jumps.
- **Wake-up OD DP (V1 16:26).** You have time to see how close they are. If they're not in range, buffer it and don't release it.
- **Wake-up supers (V1 17:04).** Wake-up Level 1/3 that worked had luck in it. Blocked, you take big damage, Drive damage and lose your meter. Usually wake up with OD DP or just down-back.
- **Down-back on wake-up after annoying exchanges (V1 22:38).** He often down-backs on wake-up when he expects the opponent to bait a reversal. If he sees them walk back to bait, he answers with 2MK~Drive Rush.
- **Hesitant to OD DP a far-range special (V1 46:48).** If you can't safely reversal it, just block it out; if they go minus it's your turn (e.g. block the low, react to the overhead). Worst case they need many more of those to kill you.
- **Parry discipline (V1 33:39).** Don't parry something you can block unless you're reacting to a specific follow-up. Example: Viper's standing HP is minus on its own and only plus with the feint, so react to whether she feints.

## How to apply this in a replay review
1. **Walk-forward check:** after the opponent "knocks on the door" (a poke that touches {{PLAYER_NAME}}), did {{PLAYER_NAME}} answer with a stationary button that whiffed or got whiff-punished, or walk in first? Count jump approaches vs walk-forward approaches, and note jumps that got anti-aired or hit by a fireball.
2. **Turtle check:** find stretches where {{PLAYER_NAME}} didn't act for 10+ seconds or down-backed after a scary exchange. Was it strategic patience (blocked out a string, took his turn when they went minus) or giving up initiative?
3. **Clue log:** after each dropped combo, low-reward hit or missed whiff punish by the opponent, log what they did next (jump, extra button, whiff, reversal) and whether {{PLAYER_NAME}} was ready. Do the same for {{PLAYER_NAME}}: what did he do after his own frustrating moments, and was it readable?
4. **Turn check:** flag throws after minus lights midscreen, point-blank unsafe normals, Drive Rush 2LK after a throw, and forced specials when not plus enough.
5. **Cammy check:** after drills, did {{PLAYER_NAME}} walk forward or use a plus button before his follow-up? Any round-start Drive Rushes? Midscreen throw followed by Drive Rush (fake) vs dash back+MP? Cannon Spike anti-airs against characters who like jumping or burn kicks?
6. **Corner check:** after {{PLAYER_NAME}} corners the opponent, did he suffocate them (walk forward, buttons, strike/throw) or walk back and let them out? Any unnecessary jump-ins in winning corner positions?
7. **Committal tools:** list round-start hard reads, DIs, wake-up supers and wake-up OD DPs, with outcome and whether they were reads or gambles.
